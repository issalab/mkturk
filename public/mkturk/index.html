Behavioral measurements of visual object recognition abilities in Healthy Adults

Informed Consent Form

We invite you to participate in a research study investigating hypotheses about human visual perception. In this study, you will be asked to watch images or videos and perform simple tasks.

Confidentiality

Although every effort will be made to protect the confidentiality of your records, absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. According to the rules governing research procedures at Columbia University, by agreeing to participate in a study, you grant permission for information about you obtained during the study to be made available to:

  • The investigator and members of their study team.
  • Authorities at Columbia University including the CUIMC Institutional Review Board (IRB) who independently review studies to assure adequate protection of research participants, as required by federal regulations.
  • The Federal Office of Human Research Protections (OHRP) and other government agencies that oversee the safety of human subjects.

Please note that fully anonymized behavioral records may be shared via open scientific repositories. Such data will not include any personally identifiable information.

Risks and Benefits

There are no personal benefits to completing this study other than the associated monetary compensation. However, your participation will provide information which may increase our understanding of human visual perception.

The behavioral testing is non-invasive and there are no known risks, other than the risk of loss of private information. This risk always exists, but there are procedures in place to minimize it.

Should you become uncomfortable at any time, you may discontinue your participation. Other than not receiving the compensation for participating in this particular research study, you will suffer no penalty.

Compensation

If this study involves the opportunity to earn rewards based on performance, you will receive this amount only after you return any lab hardware, if provided.

Tax law may require the Columbia University Finance Department to report the amount of payment you receive from Columbia University to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) or other agencies, as applicable. Generally, this reporting would take place if you receive payments that equal $600 or more from Columbia University in a calendar year. You would be responsible for the payment of any tax that may be due. You will be paid for your participation at the posted rate.

Contact Information

Taking part in this study is your choice. You can decide not to take part in or stop being in the study at any time for any reason (even after you agree to this consent form). However, even if you decide to revoke your consent and authorization, the researchers may continue to use and disclose the information they have already collected. Your decision not to participate or to withdraw from the study will not affect your future status with this investigator. At the time of your withdrawal/completion of the study, you agree to return any lab-provided hardware, and you understand that you will receive compensation only after returning any lab-provided hardware.

If you have any questions or concerns about this study, please contact any member of the research team, including the Principal Investigator, Dr. Elias Issa, PhD +1-212-853-1164.

If you have any questions or concerns about your rights or welfare as a research subject, please do not hesitate to contact the Columbia University – Medical Center Institutional Review Board (IRB). The Institutional Review Board is a committee organized to protect the rights and welfare of human subjects involved in research and operates independent of the research team.
